# Jared Earl Hazleton (1937–2026): Comprehensive Research Profile

## TL;DR
- **Jared Earl Hazleton (September 12, 1937 – September 3, 2026)** was an American economist and educator who led the Texas Research League, served as an executive at Mesa Petroleum, directed Texas A&M's Center for Business and Economic Analysis, and — per the University of Texas Press author bio — "served for five years as Dean of the University of North Texas College of Business." His website **texecon.com** is now a memorial maintained by his son, technologist Mark Hazleton.
- His most-cited scholarly works are *The Economics of the Sulphur Industry* (1970, Resources for the Future / Johns Hopkins Press, based on his Rice Ph.D. thesis) and *Managing Macroeconomic Policy: The Johnson Presidency* (1986, University of Texas Press, with James E. Anderson).
- Important disambiguation: he is a distinct person from telecom economist **Thomas W. Hazlett** and author **Lesley Hazleton**; and texecon.com's regional "economic analysis" pages are largely his son Mark's web-development demonstrations, not Jared's own writing.

## Key Findings
- Core biography is confirmed across multiple independent sources (Dallas Morning News and Austin American-Statesman/Legacy obituaries, Fort Worth Report remembrance, and a Prabook/Marquis Who's Who profile).
- texecon.com is currently a memorial. Historically it was a personal/consulting web project built and run by his son Mark Hazleton, explicitly described as part of a portfolio of technical demonstrations.
- A verified publication list spans sulphur economics, fisheries economics, macroeconomic-policy history, and Texas energy/transport policy.
- Documented public-service roles and at least one instance of Texas legislative testimony, plus an archived 1986 video interview.
- Several gaps remain: the exact Ph.D. year is unresolved, and a couple of attributed reports are not fully verified.

## Details

### Biography
Jared Earl Hazleton was born September 12, 1937, in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, the son of Alfred Larson Hazleton and Myrtle Frances Hazleton. He married Elaine Upton on November 27, 1959 — a marriage of 66 years. He died at home in the Fort Worth/Arlington, Texas, area on September 3, 2026, at age 88. He was a Navy veteran and was active in Rotary and the United Way; in college he belonged to Beta Theta Pi and Beta Gamma Sigma. He is survived by his wife Elaine; children Frances Hazleton (Lisa Matthews), Alan Hazleton (Tracy), and Mark Hazleton (Lesley); five grandchildren (Weston, Kendall, Marlis, Berit, Ian); and a sister, Susan Swingen. A Celebration of Life was held Saturday, September 19, 2026, at Celebration Community Church, 908 Pennsylvania Avenue, Fort Worth.

### Education
- Bachelor of Business Administration, University of Oklahoma, Norman, 1959. At OU he served as Vice-President of the Student Senate and participated in ROTC.
- Ph.D. in Economics, Rice University, Houston. (Prabook/Marquis list "1961," but this date appears too early given his 1959 BBA and 1959–1961 Navy service, and given that his 1970 book is described as a revision of his Rice thesis; the actual award was most plausibly mid-to-late 1960s. This remains unresolved — see Caveats.)

### Career timeline
- **1959–1961**: Officer, U.S. Navy (Lieutenant, Supply Corps, Naval Reserve), aboard the destroyer escort USS Howard D. Crow.
- **1964–1968**: Federal Reserve Bank of Boston — research economist (1964–65), manager of the research department (1965–66), banking services officer (1966–68).
- **1968–1980**: University of Texas at Austin — lecturer (1968–69), associate professor (1970–75), professor to associate dean (1975–80).
- International work with the Ford Foundation in Amman, Jordan (per obituary).
- **1980–1982**: University of Washington, Seattle — professor to dean.
- **1982–1987**: President, Texas Research League, Austin.
- **1987–1989**: Vice President for Economics, Mesa Limited Partnership / Mesa Petroleum, Amarillo.
- **1989–1999**: Texas A&M University, College Station — Director, Center for Business and Economic Analysis, and professor of finance.
- **1999–2007**: Dean, College of Business Administration, University of North Texas, Denton (the UT Press author bio characterizes this as "five years as Dean").
- **2007 onward**: Principal, TexEcon (consulting).

### Publications
- **Hazleton, Jared E.** *The Economics of the Sulphur Industry.* Baltimore: Johns Hopkins Press for Resources for the Future, 1970 (xi, 172 pp.; ISBN 0801810744). A revision of his Rice University thesis. Per the publisher's description, "Hazleton focuses on the Frasch sulphur industry to explore issues such as competing sources of sulphur, the possibilities of sulphur being obtained as a result of pollution-abating policies and the conditions under which future supplies are likely to become available." Reissued by Routledge on March 22, 2016 as a Routledge Revivals reprint of 190 pages (ISBN 1317353935 / 9781317353935; also 9781138955776).
- **Anderson, James E., and Jared E. Hazleton.** *Managing Macroeconomic Policy: The Johnson Presidency.* Austin: University of Texas Press, 1986 (xii, 285 pp.; $27.50). DOI 10.7560/750845. University of Texas Press describes it as "the first comprehensive treatment of presidential management of such policy for any presidency," in which the authors "focus on four tasks: developing and maintaining an information and decision-making system; coordination of policies in different macroeconomic areas; building support or consent for presidential policies; and administrative leadership." Part of the Administrative History of the Johnson Presidency Series (LBJ School of Public Affairs), funded in part by the NEH. The ebook/Kindle edition was published by University of Texas Press on November 6, 2014 (ASIN B00PBCY86Y). Reviewed in the *Journal of American History* (1987) and *American Historical Review* (1988); the *Journal of Economic History* review (© 1987 Economic History Association) was written by F. Gerard Adams of the University of Pennsylvania. Opening chapter: "1. The President as Economic Manager," pp. 1–10 (DOI 10.7560/750845-003).
- **Hazleton, Jared E., and Frederick W. Bell.** *Recent Developments and Research in Fisheries Economics.* 1967 (papers from a 1965 fisheries economics conference; ~233 pp.). Publisher imprint not definitively verified.
- **Hazleton, Jared E.** *Feasibility Analysis of a Regional Energy Development Bank: Final Report.* Austin: Texas Energy Advisory Council, Energy Development Fund, Report no. EDF-021, 1979.
- **Hazleton, Jared E.** *Motor Carrier Entry Regulation in Texas, No. 24.* (Likely an LBJ School of Public Affairs Policy Research Project report, UT Austin; full imprint and year unverified.)
- **Hazleton, Jared E.** "The Peso Devaluation in Retrospect." Online essay hosted by LANIC/LLILAS, University of Texas at Austin, c. 1995 (original URL has since migrated).

His published work is summarized as addressing the sulphur industry, fisheries economics, macroeconomic policy, development in oil-rich countries, environmental policy, land reform, year-round schooling, bank mergers, and regional economic stability.

### The texecon.com website
texecon.com is now a memorial to Jared Hazleton. The site is built and maintained by his son **Mark Hazleton**, a solutions architect based in the Wichita, Kansas / Roanoke, Texas area, who describes it as part of his portfolio of technical demonstrations (built in React 19/TypeScript/Tailwind, hosted on GitHub Pages). The current site includes regional "economic analysis" sections for Texas, Kansas, and Arizona, plus a "Mark Hazleton" bio page. These regional pages appear to be primarily web-development demonstration content rather than Jared Hazleton's own economic writing — a key point for anyone treating the site as a primary source for his scholarship.

# Consolidated Genealogical Report: The Ancestry of Jared Earl Hazleton (1937–2026)

## TL;DR
- **Jared Earl Hazleton's parents are now firmly documented as Alfred Larson Hazleton and Myrtle Frances (Smith) Hazleton** (married 59 years); Myrtle was born 24 Jul 1915 in Fort Smith, Arkansas, and died August 1999. His paternal grandmother was a **Larson** — a sister of Oscar Frederick Larson (1879–1954) — who married a man recorded only as **"A.A. Hazleton"** of Oklahoma City; the Larsons were Swedish Methodists of Scranton, Kansas, whose immigrant patriarch pastored the church there.
- **The maternal Smith–Goode lines rest almost entirely on family photo-album captions, not yet on records.** The reported chain (Myrtle ← O.E. Smith Sr. & Besse Goode Smith ← Benjamin Tillman Smith / W.H. Goode / John Goode) is internally consistent and plausible but remains UNVERIFIED against censuses and graves.
- **Jared's marriage to Elaine lasted 66 years, which places the wedding in 1960, not 1959**; his Rice Ph.D. year is still unconfirmed (the "1961" in Marquis/Prabook is almost certainly too early). This report supersedes all earlier reports.

## Key Findings

**1. The 66-year marriage fixes the wedding at 1960, not 1959.** Jared's 2026 obituary (Austin American-Statesman / Dallas Morning News, Legacy.com ids 62383572 / 62402686) states verbatim: "it was at OU that he met the love of his life, Elaine. Their college romance became a marriage of 66 years." 2026 − 66 = 1960. This is decisive against the reported "27 Nov 1959" date, though a late-1959 engagement or a wedding in the first days of 1960 cannot be excluded. Elaine's maiden name is independently corroborated as **Upton** by the Prabook/Marquis Who's Who entry, which lists "Spouse: Elaine Upton."

**2. Myrtle's parentage and vitals are confirmed by her 1999 Daily Oklahoman obituary.** The obituary reads: "HAZLETON Myrtle Francis, born July 24, 1915, Fort Smith, Arkansas, died August 1999. She was preceded in death by her husband of 59 years, Alfred Larson Hazleton." It names son Jared Earl Hazleton (and wife Elaine, then of Denton, Texas), daughter Susan Hazleton Swingen (husband Ron, Portland), and grandchildren including the Parr children of Susan's prior marriage. This is the first independent, near-primary confirmation of Myrtle's maiden name (SMITH), her exact birth data, and the 59-year marriage. Notably, her services were held at Cook-Walden Forest Oaks Funeral Home in the Austin, Texas area (visitation 2 Aug 1999; services 3 Aug 1999), which suggests she may have been buried near her son in Texas rather than in Oklahoma City — a lead for locating Alfred's burial as well.

**3. The paternal grandmother was a Larson.** The Carnegie Herald obituary (April 1954) of Oscar Frederick Larson names "also one sister, Mrs. A.A. Hazleton, Oklahoma City." This makes Jared's paternal grandmother a Larson and elegantly explains why Jared's father carried the middle name "Larson." The obituary further establishes the family's ethnic and religious origin: "Oscar Frederick Larson was born in 1879 in Scranton, Kan. He joined the Swedish Methodist church at the age of 12. His father was pastor of the church."

**4. The Alfred Sr./Jr. namesake hypothesis is contradicted by the initials.** The grandfather is recorded as "A.A." Hazleton — first initial A, second initial A — whereas Jared's father is Alfred **L**arson Hazleton (A.L.). The second initials differ, so a clean Alfred-Sr./Alfred-Jr. identity is NOT supported by the record; "Larson" is plainly the mother's maiden surname deployed as the son's middle name. The grandfather's first name may or may not have been "Alfred"; his second given name began with A, not L. This corrects the working hypothesis in the prior report.

**5. The Swedish Larson origin is now narrowly localizable.** Oscar Frederick Larson was born 1879 in Scranton, Osage County, Kansas; came to Oklahoma in 1901 with his brother Charlie; homesteaded near Oak Dale, Caddo County; and married Minnie A. Hileman on 5 Mar 1905. His father — the unnamed Swedish Methodist pastor and Jared's great-great-grandfather — was ministering in Scranton by the late 1870s. This pins the search for the immigrant patriarch to the Swedish Methodist Episcopal congregation of Scranton/Osage County, Kansas, in the 1870s–1880s.

### The Elizabeth Goode question (Part C)
Two scenarios remain open, and the evidence gathered does not yet decide between them:
- **Scenario 1 (one woman):** "Elizabeth Goode" = "Besse/Bessie M. Goode Smith," because *Bessie* is a standard diminutive of *Elizabeth*. Under this reading, photo 001's label of Jared as her "grandson" is literally correct (Besse = Jared's maternal grandmother), and the apparent generational contradiction disappears.
- **Scenario 2 (two women):** Elizabeth is W.H. Goode's wife (Besse's mother), making her Jared's **great-grandmother**, with "grandson" used loosely/affectionately.
The generational chronology (John Goode b. 1844 → W.H. Goode b. ~1870s → Besse b. ~1890s → Myrtle b. 1915) is internally consistent at roughly 25-year spacing under either scenario and cannot by itself resolve the question. **Best-reasoned conclusion:** Scenario 1 is the more economical reading of the caption set and should be treated as the leading hypothesis, but confirmation requires a census or grave record explicitly naming Besse's mother. Until then, both scenarios must stand.

### Jared's own record and career
His obituary confirms the OU BBA (1959; Beta Theta Pi, Beta Gamma Sigma, VP Student Senate, ROTC), Navy service as an officer aboard the destroyer escort USS Howard D. Crow, and — verbatim — that "He later joined Texas A&M University as Director of the Center for Business and Economic Analysis and professor of finance. In 1999, he became Dean of the College of Business Administration at the University of North Texas," continuing in retirement "through TexEcon, his consulting firm." The obituary also notes he "worked internationally with the Ford Foundation in Amman, Jordan," and "served as President of the Texas Research League and Vice President for Economics at Mesa Petroleum."

The Prabook/Marquis Who's Who profile supplies the granular career chronology: "Research economist Federal Reserve Bank Boston, 1964—1965, manager research department, 1965—1966, banking services officer, 1966—1968"; "Lecturer University Texas, Austin, 1968—1969 ... associate professor, 1970—1975 ... professor to associate dean, 1975—1980"; "Professor to dean University Washington, Seattle, 1980—1982"; "president, Texas Research League Austin, 1982—1987"; and "vice president, Mesa Ltd Partnership Amarillo, Texas, 1987—1989." It records his honors: "Recipient, John W. Gardner award, Rice University, 1965, Red Feather Award, Red Feather Society, 2004; grant, National Science Foundation, 1972-1976, Environmental Protection Agency, 1996-1998."

**The Rice Ph.D. year remains unresolved and the Marquis figure is suspect.** Prabook (from Marquis) states verbatim: "Doctor of Philosophy in Economics, Rice University, Houston, 1961." This is almost certainly too early: Jared was on active Navy duty 1959–61 and did not join the Federal Reserve Bank of Boston until 1964, and he received Rice's John W. Gardner award in 1965. A completion date in the early-to-mid 1960s (most plausibly 1963–1966) is far more consistent with the timeline; ProQuest Dissertations and Rice's Fondren Library catalog should be consulted to fix it precisely.
