# The Family of Dr. Jared Earl Hazleton

## A Documented Family History

**Prepared and updated September 14, 2026**

### September 12, 1937 – September 3, 2026

---

## Introduction

The family history of Dr. Jared Earl Hazleton reaches well beyond the professional biography of a distinguished economist, educator, university dean, public-policy scholar, Navy veteran, and business executive.

It is also the story of several families whose paths converged in Oklahoma: the **Hazletons, Smiths, Goodes, Byrneses, and Uptons**.

The documentary record is uneven. Jared's adult professional life is exceptionally well documented through university archives, published scholarship, institutional records, newspapers, and government documents. The family story relies on a mixture of public records, obituaries, municipal records, contemporary newspapers, and a valuable collection of family photographs whose handwritten or remembered captions preserve names and relationships that do not always appear elsewhere.

This history therefore distinguishes among three kinds of evidence:

**Documented facts** are supported by contemporary public or institutional records.

**Family-source evidence** comes from obituaries, family photographs, captions, and preserved family knowledge. These sources can be highly valuable, particularly when identifying relatives, but should ideally be corroborated by official records.

**Working hypotheses** are plausible connections that remain unproven and should not yet be incorporated into a permanent genealogical tree as established fact.

This distinction is especially important when researching common names such as Smith and variant spellings such as **Hazleton/Hazelton**, **Besse/Bessie**, and **Frances/Francis**.

The result is not yet the final word on the Hazleton family.

It is the most complete defensible reconstruction available from the evidence assembled so far.

# Generation One of the documented Hazleton line

## Alfred Larson Hazleton and Myrtle Frances Smith Hazleton

The immediate family story begins with **Alfred Larson Hazleton** and **Myrtle Frances Smith Hazleton**, Jared's parents.

Jared's funeral-home record identifies his parents as **Alfred Larson Hazleton** and **Myrtle Francis**. The Smith family photograph collection supplies the more complete identification **Myrtle Frances Smith Hazleton** and explicitly identifies her as the daughter of **O.E. Smith Sr. and Besse Smith**.

This is an important genealogical advance.

Earlier research had been able to identify Myrtle only by her married name and could not establish her maiden name. The family photograph captions now connect her directly to the **Smith family** and, through her mother Besse, to the **Goode family**.

The spelling of Myrtle's middle name varies between **Francis** and **Frances**. Because Frances is more commonly a woman's given name and appears in the family-album material, **Myrtle Frances Smith Hazleton** is the strongest working form, but an original birth, marriage, or death record should ultimately determine the legal spelling.

Alfred's middle name is consistently preserved as **Larson**.

Unlike Myrtle's ancestry, Alfred's ancestry remains one of the largest unanswered questions in the family tree.

No record yet located identifies Alfred's parents with sufficient certainty.

---

# Alfred and Myrtle in Oklahoma City

Alfred and Myrtle established their family in **Oklahoma City, Oklahoma**.

Their son **Jared Earl Hazleton** was born there on September 12, 1937.

Their daughter **Susan Hazleton**, later **Susan Hazleton Swingen**, was Jared's sister.

A 1956 Oklahoma City newspaper refers to a **Mrs. Alfred L. Hazelton** serving as a district chairman in a citywide women's civic campaign. The combination of location, husband, period, and surname makes it highly likely that this was Myrtle.

If that identification is correct, it gives us a rare glimpse of Myrtle as more than an entry in a family tree.

She appears to have been participating actively in Oklahoma City civic life while her son Jared was attending the University of Oklahoma.

The spelling **Hazelton** in contemporary Oklahoma records should not be treated as evidence of a different family. Jared himself appears repeatedly in 1950s Oklahoma newspapers as **Jared Hazelton**, while his adult professional career consistently used **Hazleton**.

Both spellings must therefore be searched when researching this family.

# Elizabeth Goode: an unresolved relationship

One of the most interesting contradictions in the album concerns **Elizabeth Goode**.

Photo 001 describes Jared as **Elizabeth Goode's grandson**.

Another photograph names Elizabeth and Jared together but does not explain the relationship.

Taken literally, “grandmother” would place Elizabeth one generation above Jared's mother.

But the established working line currently runs:

```text
Jared
   |
Myrtle
   |
Besse
   |
W.H. Goode
```

If Besse was Myrtle's mother, then W.H.'s wife would be Myrtle's grandmother and Jared's **great-grandmother**, not grandmother.

Therefore, Elizabeth cannot simply be assumed to have been W.H. Goode's wife.

Several explanations are possible:

* the caption may use “grandmother” loosely;
* Elizabeth may belong to another branch of Jared's family;
* a generation may be misunderstood in the current tree;
* or Elizabeth may have been a step-grandmother or another close relative remembered as a grandmother.

No conclusion should be forced.

**Elizabeth Goode remains an open genealogical problem.**

# Jared Earl Hazleton

## Birth and childhood

**Jared Earl Hazleton** was born September 12, 1937, in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, to Alfred Larson Hazleton and Myrtle Frances Smith Hazleton.

He grew up there with his sister Susan.

The public record becomes surprisingly detailed during his teenage years.

Jared attended **Classen High School**.

In January 1955, an Oklahoma City newspaper reported that **Jared Hazelton of Classen** had won recognition in men's extemporaneous speaking.

The record is significant because public speaking, analysis, and argument would later become central skills in his life as a teacher, economist, administrator, consultant, and public-policy leader.

---

# The University of Oklahoma

Later in 1955 Jared entered the **University of Oklahoma**.

An October 1955 Oklahoma newspaper identifies **Jared E. Hazelton** among new Naval ROTC contract students at OU.

The NROTC program was not a casual extracurricular activity. Students qualified through a competitive process and committed to later Naval service.

In December 1955, *The Oklahoma Daily* reported that **Jared Hazelton, an Oklahoma City freshman**, competed for the university debate squad.

Jared and teammate Bill Carr achieved a winning record at a Southwestern College tournament, and both received excellent ratings in discussion.

These records show that, even as an eighteen-year-old freshman, Jared had already begun developing three characteristics that would remain evident throughout his life:

**public speaking, analytical argument, and leadership.**

He later became active in:

**Beta Theta Pi**

**Beta Gamma Sigma**

**Student Senate**, where he served as vice president

and

**ROTC/NROTC**

He graduated from OU in **1959**.

Family records identify his degree as a **Bachelor of Business Administration**, while a later professional biography specifies that his undergraduate field was **accounting**.

The safest formulation is:

**B.B.A., University of Oklahoma, 1959, with studies in accounting.**

# Jared and Elaine's marriage

A later research report gives:

# Naval service and young married life

After graduating from OU, Jared served as an officer in the United States Navy.

Family records say he served aboard the destroyer escort:

**USS Howard D. Crow**

His service appears to have lasted approximately **1959–1961**.

That period coincided with the early years of his marriage to Elaine.

His later professional biographies identify him as a Navy officer before he began advanced studies in economics.

Those years marked the transition from Oklahoma student to husband, Naval officer, and ultimately scholar.

# Rice University

After Naval service, Jared pursued doctoral studies in economics at **Rice University**.

His dissertation examined the sulphur industry and later became the basis for his book:

**The Economics of the Sulphur Industry**

published in 1970.

The exact Ph.D. year produced conflicting information in later sources.

TexEcon listed **1961**.

Other family materials used **1964**.

The strongest contemporary evidence located so far is a **1987 professional newspaper profile**, published during Jared's lifetime when he joined Mesa, which states that he received his **doctorate in economics from Rice University in 1965**.

That date also fits the separate family/professional reference to the **John W. Gardner Award in 1965**.

Unless an original Rice diploma, commencement program, transcript, or registrar record proves otherwise, the best current working date is therefore:

**Ph.D. in Economics, Rice University, 1965.**

# A family story across generations

The available evidence now allows a much fuller story than simply saying Jared Hazleton was born in Oklahoma City.

On his mother's side, he appears to descend from families with deep roots in Oklahoma and the surrounding region.

The **Goodes** include John and Susan, born in the middle of the nineteenth century and apparently buried near Edmond.

Their descendant **Besse Goode** married **O.E. Smith Sr.**

The Smith family appears to have been entrepreneurial and close-knit, operating cleaning businesses and maintaining strong ties among siblings, cousins, uncles, grandparents, and grandchildren.

Besse and O.E.'s daughter **Myrtle Frances Smith** married **Alfred Larson Hazleton**.

They raised Jared and Susan in Oklahoma City.

Jared grew up in a household connected not only to the Hazleton name but to a broad Smith family network whose members repeatedly appear together in photographs, holidays, businesses, cemeteries, and family stories.

As a teenager, Jared was already speaking competitively and debating.

At the University of Oklahoma, he entered Naval ROTC, studied business and accounting, participated in student leadership, and met **Elaine Upton**.

Elaine brought her own family heritage into the story through **Wesley Chandler Upton and Laura Brown Upton**.

Jared and Elaine married around the time he completed college.

They began their adult lives through Navy service and graduate education.

Then came Massachusetts and the Federal Reserve, where surviving municipal records show them raising a young family in Bedford.

They returned to Texas.

They lived internationally during the Jordan years.

They moved to Seattle for Jared's first major deanship.

Then they returned again to Texas, passing through Austin, Amarillo, College Station, and North Texas as Jared moved between public policy, business, economics, teaching, and university leadership.

Their children grew into adults and formed families of their own.

By the time Jared died in 2026, the family included three children, their spouses, five grandchildren, his sister Susan, nieces, nephews, and a much larger extended network reaching back through the Smith, Goode, Byrnes, Upton, Brown, and Hazleton families.
